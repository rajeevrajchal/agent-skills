# Market Research

Research only the ideas that survived screening. Aim to answer **"is there room for a small, focused product, and where?"**, not to produce an encyclopedia.

## Research procedure

For each surviving idea:
1. **Map the alternatives** (8–15 found, 3–6 analyzed in depth): direct competitors, indirect competitors, horizontal tools used for the job (spreadsheets, Notion, Airtable, generic CRMs), services and agencies, open-source tools, and "do nothing".
2. **Collect the facts** on each in-depth competitor (table below), from their own site, pricing page, changelog, and docs. Date everything.
3. **Mine user voice**: reviews and discussions (below). Group the complaints into themes and count how often each appears.
4. **Read the market direction**: growth, saturation, consolidation (below).
5. **Synthesize**: what the incumbents do well, what they do badly, which segments they ignore, and where the opportunity is. End with a 3–5 sentence "opportunity hypothesis" labelled [R] or [A].

Use parallel searches for independent competitors. Fetch the primary pages (pricing, features) rather than relying on comparison listicles, which are often outdated or affiliate-driven.

## What to collect per competitor

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Name, URL, date checked | |
| Target customer and positioning | Their headline and who their case studies feature |
| Core jobs covered | What it actually does for the problem in question |
| Pricing | Tiers, entry price, free plan, per-seat vs flat, annual discounts |
| Business model | SaaS, one-time, usage-based, services |
| Strengths | What users praise (from reviews) |
| Weaknesses | Repeated complaints (from reviews and forums) |
| Traction signals | Review count, team size, funding, job posts, app installs, changelog activity |
| Recent moves | New features, price changes, acquisitions, pivots |
| Distribution | How they acquire users: SEO, marketplace, ads, sales, community |

## Finding competitors

Queries:
- `<problem in user's words>` (what users would actually type)
- `<job> software`, `<job> app`, `<job> tool for <segment>`
- `<known competitor> alternatives`, `<known competitor> vs`
- `best <category> for <segment> <current year>`
- Marketplace searches: G2/Capterra categories, Shopify/WordPress/Chrome/Figma/Slack app directories, Product Hunt, GitHub topics (open source)
- `site:news.ycombinator.com "Show HN" <category>` and Indie Hackers product listings for recent indie entrants

A search that turns up no competitors usually means the wrong search terms, or no demand. Rarely does it mean a blue ocean. Try the users' language and adjacent categories before concluding.

## Mining reviews and discussions

Sources: G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, the App Store and Google Play, marketplace reviews, Reddit, Hacker News, niche forums, YouTube comments on tutorials, X/LinkedIn posts.

Procedure:
1. Collect 20–50 negative or mixed reviews and posts across competitors (1–3 stars is where the gaps are).
2. Tag each with themes: price, complexity, a missing feature, support, performance, integrations, onboarding, wrong-fit segment.
3. Count the themes. Note **who** complains (segment) and **what they switched to**.
4. Pull 3–5 representative quotes with links for the report.

Themes that point at opportunities for small products: "too expensive for small teams or solo users", "too complex, we only need X", "doesn't support <region/language/regulation>", "no integration with <tool>", "great but not built for <niche>".

## Market direction signals

| Signal | Suggests |
|---|---|
| New entrants in the last 12–24 months, rising search interest, funding rounds, job posts | Growing market; validate differentiation |
| Many near-identical products, aggressive discounting, review sites dominated by ads | Saturated; you need a sharp niche or distribution edge |
| Acquisitions and consolidation, incumbents raising prices | Possible openings for cheaper and simpler alternatives |
| Product shutdowns or deprecations | Orphaned users looking for a replacement (time-limited window) |
| Platform owners shipping the feature natively | Shrinking opportunity for standalone tools |
| Regulation deadlines | Time-boxed demand spike; check the enforcement dates |

Use Google Trends-style interest data and keyword tools where available. Treat them as directional, not exact.

## Sizing for a solo founder

Skip TAM theatre. Estimate:
1. **Reachable segment**: how many potential buyers can you actually reach through your channels? (Community size, directory listings, search volume, number of businesses of that type in the target region.)
2. **Required customers**: the goal revenue ÷ the realistic price. For example, €5,000 MRR ÷ €29 ≈ 170 customers.
3. **Plausibility**: a required share of under about 1–2% of the reachable segment is comfortable; above 10% is a red flag.

Label each input [F], [R], or [A], and show the arithmetic.

## Evidence log

Keep every source in the ledger with: claim, label, source URL, date accessed, and a quote or number. The final report cites from this log. This stops facts and assumptions blurring together over a long research session.

## Pitfalls

- Listicles and "top 10" articles are often stale or affiliate-driven. Verify on the primary site.
- Review counts on G2/Capterra favor companies that run review campaigns. Weight the content over the counts.
- Reddit skews technical and vocal. Cross-check with other sources for non-technical segments.
- Prices in different currencies and billing periods: normalize to the monthly price in one currency, and note seats.
- Don't confuse traffic or downloads with revenue.
