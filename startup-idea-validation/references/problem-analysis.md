## Root-problem worksheet

For each idea, fill this in before any market research:

| Question | Answer | Evidence label |
|---|---|---|
| Who has the problem? (segment, role, company size, region) | | |
| What is the problem, in their words? | | |
| How often does it occur? (daily / weekly / monthly / yearly / once) | | |
| How painful or expensive is it? Quantify it (hours, €, risk, missed revenue) | | |
| What do they do today? | | |
| Why is that insufficient? | | |
| What triggers the search for a solution? | | |
| Who pays, and from which budget? | | |
| Root cause: why does the problem exist? (Ask "why?" until you hit something structural) | | |

**The 5-whys test**: if the root cause is "they lack tool X", keep asking why. Often the real root is a process, a skill, or an incentive problem that software can't fix, or it's a different and better problem.

## Jobs-to-be-done framing

> When [situation/trigger], I want to [motivation/job], so I can [expected outcome].

Separate:
- **Functional job** (send invoices on time).
- **Emotional job** (not feel anxious about cash flow).
- **Social job** (look professional to clients).

Products that serve the functional job and the emotional job together tend to retain better. The trigger tells you **when** and **where** to reach users (see `roadmap-and-gtm.md`).

## Gates (knock-out checks)

Any **Fail** means ❌ Reject. Any **Unknown** caps the verdict at ⚠️ Needs validation until it's resolved.

| Gate | Pass when |
|---|---|
| **G1 Real, specific problem** | A specific segment and a concrete, recurring or high-stakes problem can be named, with some evidence beyond the founder's own opinion |
| **G2 Reachable buyers** | At least one plausible channel to reach buyers that the founder can use (a community, search intent, a marketplace, a network, outbound to an identifiable list) |
| **G3 Someone pays** | An identifiable payer with a budget or a history of spending on alternatives (money, or substantial time that converts to money) |
| **G4 Feasible for this founder** | An MVP is buildable within the founder's skills, time, and budget (allowing for learning), with no capital-intensive dependency |
| **G5 Acceptable legal and platform risk** | No licensing or compliance wall the founder can't clear, and no total dependence on a platform likely to forbid or clone it |

## Quick screen

For the 10–50 ideas from discovery, without deep research, rate each 1–5 on: **pain**, **frequency**, **payer clarity**, **reachability for this founder**, and **founder fit**. Check the gates using reasoning and quick searches only.

- Keep ideas with gates passing (or unknown) and a total of at least 15/25.
- Merge near-duplicates.
- Record the rejected ones with a one-line reason in the ledger.

This prevents expensive research on weak ideas.

## Customer conversation guide

Desk research can't replace talking to people who have the problem. Good questions ask about **past behavior**, not opinions about the future (the principles of *The Mom Test*):

Ask:
- "Tell me about the last time you had to [job]. What happened?"
- "How do you handle it today? Walk me through it."
- "What have you tried? What did you pay for? Why did you stop?"
- "How much time or money does this cost you each month?"
- "Who else is involved? Who decides on tools and budget?"
- "What would happen if you didn't solve it at all?"

Avoid:
- "Would you use / pay for an app that…?" (People are polite; future promises are worthless.)
- Pitching the idea before understanding the problem.
- Leading questions ("Isn't it frustrating that…?").

Strong signals:
- They've already built a workaround, paid for a partial solution, or complain without being prompted.
- They ask when they can try it, offer to pay, or introduce colleagues (commitment and advancement).

Aim for 5–10 conversations per segment before scoring willingness to pay above "research" level. Log quotes in the ledger as evidence.

## Red flags in problem framing

- The statement contains a solution ("They need an AI app to…").
- The problem only happens once in a lifetime, with no trigger you can intercept.
- "Nice to have": nobody would notice if it were never solved.
- The pain belongs to someone who isn't the buyer, and the buyer doesn't care.
- The founder is the only known person with the problem.
- Existing free tools solve it well enough, and complaints are rare.
- The problem is already being solved by the incumbent's roadmap (check changelogs and announcements).
